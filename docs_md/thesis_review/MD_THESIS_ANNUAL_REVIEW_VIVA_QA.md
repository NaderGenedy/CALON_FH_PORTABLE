## Part 1. Pre-meeting fixes: internal inconsistencies found on the paragraph-by-paragraph read

These are the items an attentive examiner can find by reading alone. Fix them, or be ready to concede them without hesitation. Each is located by section.

**P1. Decision-curve counts differ between chapter and ledger.** Section 7.12 states the frozen CALON-5 model exceeded both default strategies at 13 of 30 thresholds into the registry and 10 of 30 out of it. **P2. **P7. ## Part 2. Things not to say in the room

These are numbers or phrases that circulate in the candidate's own working notes and earlier drafts but that the thesis either does not support or explicitly retires.

- Do not call the UK Biobank AUC of 0.756 a "transport" or "external validation" result. The thesis is explicit that 0.756 is an apparent AUC after target refitting (Table 6.3). The frozen transport value is 0.669 (0.650 to 0.687).
- Do not quote a type 2 diabetes TUDOR AUC of 0.718. The thesis reports 0.648 in Wales and 0.642 (0.559 to 0.724) in UK Biobank (Section 6.5).
- Do not lean on the statin-naive AUC of 0.801. The thesis says it "was reported without a verified denominator or confidence interval and cannot carry a strong untreated-population conclusion" (Section 6.5).
- Do not describe 0.732 and 0.770 as "bidirectional" validation of TUDOR between cohorts. They are reciprocal geographic splits within Wales (Table 6.3). The bidirectional design belongs to Chapter 7 (CALON-5).
- Do not say TUDOR "diagnoses" FH, is "ascertainment-aware" or "encodes the Index Effect". The evaluated object has no route term (Box 6.1).
- Do not say "external validation" for CALON-5. The thesis calls it "cross-cohort evaluation" because both cohorts informed development (Sections 3.8, 7.3).
- Do not quote the hazard ratio of 38.55 as an effect size. It is "formally retired" (Section 4.6).
- Do not quote the 15-year event-free survival contrast of 32% versus 84%; it is "retained only as historical output" (Section 4.3).
- Do not say the residual "causes" or "drives" events, or that lowering it would reduce risk (Section 4.5). Use "was associated with", "carried information".
- Do not say ascertainment "changes" LDL-C. Say route "changes which phenotype is seen, not the biology" (Section 5.7).
- Do not say the within-pedigree difference is "significant" without adding that the interval spans zero under the dummy-variable degrees-of-freedom correction (minus 0.06 to 0.85; Section 5.3).
- Do not say "validated prediction model" of anything. The thesis cites Van Calster 2023, "There is no such thing as a validated prediction model" (reference 99).
- Do not cite the CALON-C manuscript's Holm-adjusted comparator wins (+0.070 versus SAFEHEART-RE etc.) as thesis results; Appendix B.6 supersedes them and holds the non-inferiority analysis.
- Do not say the menopause study shows an FH-specific menopause effect (Section 8.4 says it does not).
- Do not say "the erratum corrected the discordance paper's estimates". It corrected typographical errors in the highlights only (Sections 2.5, 4.3).

## Part 3. Opening questions (first five minutes)

**Q3.1 Tell me what this thesis is about in two minutes.**

*Why asked:* to see whether the candidate owns one question rather than five papers.

*Model answer:* "It asks one question: when does the same LDL-C value mean the same thing in FH, and what must be added when it does not (Section 1.1). I tested five conditions in linked genotyped cohorts, the All-Wales FH registry and UK Biobank. First, particles: at the same LDL-C, apoB above expectation was associated with more first ASCVD events, hazard ratio 1.356 per SD in 1,461 rare LDLR/APOB variant carriers with 75 events, and a Welsh equation applied unchanged kept the association (Chapter 4). Second, route into care: at matched LDLR residues, probands had untreated LDL-C 1.09 mmol/L higher than cascade-detected relatives, narrowing to 0.39 within families (Chapter 5). Third, treatment: a treatment-aware triage score, TUDOR, ranked carriers better than reconstructed criteria, AUC 0.760 versus 0.652, but fell to 0.669 when frozen in UK Biobank; and in the registry three in four carriers with a severe untreated LDL-C were graded milder on the recorded value (Chapters 6 and 7). Fourth, cohort: a five-term routine-data risk model kept its ranking in both directions between the cohorts, concordance about 0.70, but its absolute risks failed in opposite directions, observed-to-expected 1.96 and 0.70 (Chapter 7). Fifth, outside FH, the same apoB cut-point carried different absolute risk in women and men (Section 8.4). The conclusion is that LDL-C remains indispensable but should not travel alone. No model is ready for clinical use; a decisive validation needs several hundred events."

*Trap:* listing papers by journal instead of by condition.

**Q3.2 What is the single most important finding, and what is the single most important limitation?**

*Model answer:* "Most direct clinical finding: in 261 genotype-positive registry patients with directly measured untreated and on-treatment LDL-C, 145 of 193 (75.1%) who were at or above 4.9 mmol/L untreated were not above it on the recorded value, and 66.3% sat in a lower ESC/EAS stratum (Section 7.7). It needs no model and no reconstruction. Most important limitation: there is no untouched evaluation cohort. Both prognostic models were developed with knowledge of the receiving cohort, and TUDOR's transport and the related abstract used the same two cohorts (Section 8.7, limitation L04). Everything labelled transport may be optimistic to an unknown degree."

**Q3.3 Why does the title lead with "particle burden" when four of the five conditions are not about particles?**

*Model answer:* "Fair point (see Part 1, P13). The programme began with the measurement question and the title was fixed early. The thesis's own framing is the LDL-C question; particle burden is the first condition tested. I would be willing to revise to a subtitle that names the question if the committee advises."

**Q3.4 Who did the statistics?**

*Model answer:* "I did, in R and Python, with statistical supervision from Dr Aubin (Statement of original work). Chapter 5's values are those of the Paper 14 R4 manuscript and were not independently re-run for the thesis (Section 5.3); Chapter 7's are those of manuscript r7 (Section 7.1). The thesis reanalyses carry package identifiers THESIS-AN-01 to THESIS-AN-13 (Appendix A)."

## Part 4. Chapter-by-chapter question bank

### 4.1 Chapter 1: Introduction and framework

**Q1.1 Your objectives and falsifiers (Table 1.2) were, by your own account, "written across a completed programme". Isn't that hypothesising after the results are known?**

*Why asked:* HARKing; the biostatistician's first probe.

*Model answer:* "Yes, and the thesis says so three times (Sections 1.4, 1.5, 8.1). The framework is a thesis-level synthesis, not a prospective protocol. The falsifiers are useful for a different purpose: they check whether my reading of my own results is consistent, and they set the terms for the resolving studies in Section 8.8, which are prospective. I did not alter the prespecification of any source study. Where a source had a prospective analysis register, I report it: Paper 14 registered 120 analyses with BH-FDR control in fifteen families, 80 tested, 50 surviving (Section 5.2). Objective 4 was restated after CALON-5 replaced CALON-C, and that is disclosed (Section 1.5)."

*Trap:* claiming the falsifiers were prespecified.

**Q1.2 Is your "Measure to Select to Identify to Predict" arrow a causal pathway?**

*Model answer:* "No. Section 1.4 and Figure 1.1 say the arrows show the order in which the thesis reads the evidence. It is an inferential dependency: measurement uncertainty propagates into identification and prediction; selection can make a reproducible measurement non-transportable. Nothing in it says referral changes LDLR biology."

**Q1.3 Define "ascertainment-conditioned phenotype" without jargon.**

*Model answer:* "The FH phenotype as it appears in people selected by a given route into care. It is a property of the selected group, not of the variant (Glossary; Section 1.3). It is a description of how the data were generated, not an additive equation."

**Q1.4 What are the five checks that make the framework operational?**

*Model answer:* "Estimand, target population, sampling frame, measurement model and evidence for transport (Section 1.4). Every result in the thesis is supposed to carry all five."

**Q1.5 What does the thesis explicitly not claim? Give me the non-claims.**

*Model answer:* "Table 1.1 lists them: apoB does not replace LDL-C or define a target; referral does not change lipid biology and there is no individual correction factor; a score does not establish molecular diagnosis or replace sequencing; ranking does not supply portable absolute risk or treatment guidance; and the sex section is not an FH-specific or causal claim."

### 4.2 Chapter 2: Literature review

**Q2.1 This is a "critical, claim-led review" that you describe as "search incomplete". Why should I trust any of your novelty statements?**

*Model answer:* "You should read them as conditional, which is how they are written. PubMed, Crossref, Europe PMC preprints and OpenAlex were searched to 23 September 2026, and PubMed and Crossref again on 27 September with 27 queries, 339 unique records and 23 retained (Section 2.1, Appendix F.10). Embase was not searched; Elicit, Consensus, Scite and SciSpace returned no records; screening stopped at 20 to 25 records per query and was AI-assisted and single-reviewer (F.8). Every 'no study identified' sentence names those limits. The work to complete the search is listed in F.9 and is on my plan (Part 9)."

**Q2.2 Why is apoB not simply superior to LDL-C? State the strongest evidence on each side from your own review.**

*Model answer:* "For complementarity rather than superiority: the Emerging Risk Factors Collaboration found similar association shapes for apoB and non-HDL-C in 302,430 participants (Section 2.3, reference 16), and Sniderman's own INTERHEART re-analysis warned that adjustment among correlated lipids produces unstable claims of independence (reference 17). For apoB carrying information: in statin-treated cohorts apoB retained an association of 1.27 per SD when LDL-C and triglycerides did not (reference 13), discordantly high apoB carried an MI hazard ratio of 1.49 (reference 14), and at LDL-C 100 mg/dL the middle 95% of apoB spanned 66 to 99 mg/dL in NHANES (reference 15). Mendelian randomisation is split by specification (references 11 and 18). Hence 'the defensible premise is complementarity' (Section 1.2)."

**Q2.3 Why does FH make discordance uncertain rather than obvious?**

*Model answer:* "Two opposing priors (Section 2.4). Receptor impairment could tighten the mass-to-particle relation so LDL-C is an unusually faithful proxy and the residual adds nothing. Or metabolic heterogeneity, diabetes, remnants, Lp(a) and treatment coexist with the variant and leave clinically relevant variation around the relation. Equipoise was appropriate, and Chapter 4 was designed to fail if the first prior held."

**Q2.4 What is the difference between a ratio and a residual as an estimand?**

*Model answer:* "A ratio is mechanically coupled to its denominator and assumes proportionality across the whole range; adjusting the ratio for LDL-C compares a transformed variable with one of its own components (Section 2.5, references 19 and 20). A residual is observed apoB minus apoB expected at that LDL-C under a stated equation; it declares its dependence on the derivation relation, which can then be frozen and tested. They answer different questions and neither is a negative control."

**Q2.5 Name the five model states and say which one is external validation.**

*Model answer:* "Frozen application; target-adapted preprocessing; recalibration; updating; target refitting (Section 3.8). Only frozen application evaluates the source model unchanged. Refitting in the target cohort is local development, not validation (Section 2.10)."

**Q2.6 Which prior study is closest to each of your four empirical chapters, and what is left over?**

*Model answer:* "Table 2.4. Measure: Johannesen 2024 excess apoB, Pan 2026 frozen equation validated in UK Biobank outside FH; left over, application in an FH frame with incident ASCVD and a frozen Welsh equation. Select: Tybjaerg-Hansen 2005 showed the same LDLR mutations give larger LDL-C increments the more selected the population; left over, a within-service, residue-matched, within-pedigree ladder. Identify: Besseling 2017, FAMCAT, Haralambos 2015, Akyea 2026; left over, pretreatment reconstruction with a shared-data comparison and frozen application, against which Akyea 2026 is the counterweight. Predict: McKay 2022, Mansilla-Rodriguez 2025, Tamehri Zadeh 2025 and 2026; left over, bidirectional paired evaluation with ranking and calibration separated and calculability reported."

### 4.3 Chapter 3: Shared methods and governance

**Q3.1 You describe six evidence tiers, T1 to T6. Which tier carries most of the thesis?**

*Model answer:* "By claim count, T3 (submitted manuscripts: Paper 14 R4 for Chapter 5, CALON r7 for Chapter 7) and T4 (frozen thesis reanalyses: THESIS-AN-01 for Chapter 4). TUDOR's head-to-head and transport results are T2 (published). The competing-risk sensitivity in Chapter 4 and the CALON-C lineage values are T5 and are never load-bearing (Table A.2). I know which claims would fall if a T3 manuscript changed on review; Appendix B.10 shows exactly that happening to the within-pedigree rung across four Paper 14 versions."

**Q3.2 Family structure: how did you handle it in each chapter, honestly?**

*Model answer:* "Section 3.7.1. Chapter 5: pedigree-clustered standard errors, wild cluster bootstrap with 1,999 pedigree resamples, and pedigree fixed effects. Chapter 7: registry resampling grouped by family, both models scored on the same resamples. Chapter 6: not adequately. The TUDOR paired tests used DeLong, which treats relatives as independent, and no family-aware optimism estimate exists (Section 6.4, limitation L11). UK Biobank was treated as singleton clusters because the extract had no kinship field, and independence is not assumed (Section 3.7.1). The registry-side repair for TUDOR, a family-grouped bootstrap, needs no UK Biobank access and is on my plan."

**Q3.3 Why three different treatment-reconstruction conventions?**

*Model answer:* "Because three studies were built at different times for different modelling requirements (Section 3.6, Table 3.4). Convention A, intensity-specific residual fractions, for discordance and TUDOR. Convention B, population-average scalars of 0.70 and 0.80, for CALON-C. Convention C, a regression on 684 within-person registry pairs, untreated non-HDL-C = 5.2299 + 0.3345 x treated, for CALON-5. Appendix D says plainly that the record does not establish why they differ and that they must not validate one another. That is a weakness of an assembled programme, and I own it."

*Follow-up probe:* "So which one is right?" Answer: "None recovers an individual value. Convention C has cross-validated MAE 1.25 mmol/L and R-squared 0.0975 (Section 7.2); TUDOR's resource has MAE 1.20 in 649 pairs (Section 6.6); Chapter 5's reconstructed rows correlate 0.09 with documented values (Section 5.2). All three support group-level targeting and rank stability only."

**Q3.4 Explain the difference between a cause-specific hazard and a subdistribution hazard, and which you used where.**

*Model answer:* "The cause-specific hazard is the instantaneous event rate among those still event-free and is the association estimand in Chapter 4. The subdistribution hazard describes the cumulative-incidence process in the presence of competing death; a Fine-Gray-style estimate was retained in Chapter 4 only as an unverified sensitivity. Absolute incidence uses Aalen-Johansen, not one minus Kaplan-Meier, in Chapters 4 and 7 (Table 3.3, Sections 3.5, 4.2)."

**Q3.5 What is your multiplicity position?**

*Model answer:* "No programme-wide correction (Section 3.8). Estimates are read by interval and by whether they were prespecified. Where a source applied a procedure, it is reported with its family: BH-FDR across the Paper 14 register; Holm in the CALON-C lineage. In Chapter 7 I state that about one nominal interaction per family is expected by chance (Section 7.13)."

**Q3.6 What is the "no-do-harm adoption gate"?**

*Model answer:* "Section 3.9. Before any construct, score or model moves toward use it must show no material loss in calibration, stability, subgroup performance or decision utility on transport; it must not reduce testing or treatment where a known variant, pedigree or physical sign already indicates it; and it must not substitute for sequencing or guideline-directed lipid lowering. Then prospective assessment. Discrimination alone and retrospective decision curves do not pass it."

### 4.4 Chapter 4: Measure (LDL-conditioned apoB residual)

**Q4.1 Your published paper reported a hazard ratio of 38.55. Defend or retract it.**

*Model answer:* "Retracted as an effect estimate and retired formally (Section 4.6). Reasons: 20 incident events among 61 disease observations, 41 of which were prevalent; a binary exposure cross-classified with covariates, leaving near-empty cells; an interval from 3.72 to 399.36 spanning two orders of magnitude; mathematical coupling of the ratio to LDL-C; dichotomisation at a study-specific cut-point of 0.31 g/mmol; 40% apoB missingness handled by 20 imputations (Sections 4.3, 4.4). The paper called itself hypothesis-generating. The reanalysis of the same threshold in UK Biobank gave 1.24 (0.72 to 2.16), which does not support the boundary (Table 4.2). The paper still stands for the observation it made: in 14 treatment-naive pairs the ratio varied 2.1-fold, 0.18 to 0.38 g/mmol."

*Trap:* defending the magnitude or blaming the journal.

**Q4.2 What exactly is the primary estimate, and how robust is it?**

*Model answer:* "In THESIS-AN-01, 1,461 UK Biobank participants selected by an operational rare LDLR/APOB variant rule, 75 incident ASCVD events, the standardised LDL-conditioned apoB residual had an adjusted cause-specific hazard ratio of 1.356 per SD (1.070 to 1.718), adjusted for age, sex, log-triglycerides, HDL-C and diabetes. The ladder was 1.556 with age and sex, 1.469 adding triglycerides, 1.356 fully adjusted; the age-timescale delayed-entry sensitivity gave 1.467 (Table 4.2). The attenuation with metabolic covariates is consistent with shared information, which is why I do not call it a pure particle effect. Not robust in two respects: the competing-risk estimate, 1.334, is an unverified IPCW approximation and is non-load-bearing; and four of the 75 events still await procedure or death-certificate confirmation (Section 4.2)."

**Q4.3 Does the residual add anything to prediction?**

*Model answer:* "Barely, at this precision. Overall concordance 0.697; adding the residual to age and sex gave +0.050 (0.003 to 0.108), but that base model had no LDL-C. Beyond LDL-C and conventional predictors the increment was about +0.011 with no frozen interval (Section 4.5). In the CALON-F grey zone, adding log(apoB/LDL-C) gave +0.0146 (minus 0.0050 to 0.0330), and over CALON-5 the discordance term's hazard ratio was 0.79 (0.38 to 1.62) (Section 7.9). So the residual is a phenotyping refinement, not a risk calculator. The thesis says the link from measure to predict is carried forward 'as a hypothesis, not as a demonstrated increment'."

**Q4.4 The frozen Welsh equation gave 2.22 versus a local 1.356. Is the effect bigger in UK Biobank?**

*Model answer:* "No inference of heterogeneity is permitted (Section 4.8). Welsh residual scaling standardises by the Welsh SD, so one SD is a different absolute apoB departure; assays, treatment mix and event structure differ. What the 2.22 (1.40 to 3.53) shows is that the direction of the conditional apoB signal did not depend on local re-estimation. It is construct-transport of a biomarker association, not validation of a risk model: no baseline hazard, probability or threshold crossed."

**Q4.5 In the joint model apoB was 4.94 and LDL-C 0.26. Is LDL-C protective in FH?**

*Model answer:* "No. Section 4.7: with two predictors correlated this strongly each coefficient is the remaining variation after conditioning on the other, and reversal reflects unstable partitioning, not opposing biology. That model is shown precisely to explain why the residual, which separates the fitted LDL-C component from the apoB deviation, is the interpretable representation."

**Q4.6 What is the residual, biologically?**

*Model answer:* "ApoB unexplained by LDL-C under the fitted relation: it may contain cholesterol-depleted LDL, remnants, Lp(a), treatment-induced compositional change and assay effects, and the coefficient cannot apportion them (Section 4.7). ### 4.5 Chapter 5: Select (ascertainment)

**Q5.1 State the primary estimate and its estimand precisely.**

*Model answer:* "Observed difference in assigned untreated LDL-C between phenotype-referred probands and cascade-detected relatives at matched affected LDLR residues within one service, with residue fixed effects and pedigree-clustered standard errors: +1.09 mmol/L (0.83 to 1.36) in 1,100 carriers, 464 probands and 636 relatives, at 50 residues in 488 pedigrees (Section 5.3). It is not a causal effect of ascertainment; proband status is partly defined by the phenotype under study."

**Q5.2 Walk me down the attenuation ladder and tell me what each step is.**

*Model answer:* "+1.09 crude; +0.81 (0.52 to 1.09) with linear age, same carriers, an adjustment; +0.65 (0.24 to 1.07) restricted to ages 40 to 65, n 457, a change of population; +0.39 (0.05 to 0.73) with pedigree fixed effects and age, same 1,100 carriers but informed by only the 236 pedigrees containing both routes, a change of comparison (Section 5.3, Table 5.2). Probands were two decades older, median 60.2 versus 38.6 years, so age was the largest threat. The fall from crude to within-pedigree is model, not sample: on the fixed membership, pedigree effects without age gave +0.59 and with age and sex +0.40."

**Q5.3 Is the within-family difference real?**

*Model answer:* "Detectable but imprecise, and I would not claim more. p = 0.023; pedigree bootstrap 0.04 to 0.74; under a dummy-variable degrees-of-freedom correction the interval is minus 0.06 to 0.85 (Section 5.3). The conclusion depends on the variance convention. Appendix B.10 shows the rung has moved across four manuscript versions: +0.34 (0.01 to 0.67), +0.34 (minus 0.07 to 0.76), +0.40 (minus 0.003 to 0.81), +0.39. The falsifier for Objective 2 is not met, but narrowly. What the rung supports is a residual within-family association that the design cannot attribute to referral selection or biology."

*Trap:* saying "significant" and stopping.

**Q5.4 Residue matching is not allele matching. How much does that matter?**

*Model answer:* "It matters, and the chapter says residue matching 'narrows rather than solves' (Section 5.2). It blocks the explanation that groups carried variants in different receptor regions; it does not control the substitution, functional class, polygenic background, Lp(a), era or treatment. The within-pedigree rung fixes the familial allele and shared background but conditions on a selected minority of families. Leave-one-residue-out and leave-one-variant-out moved the estimate by no more than 0.13 mmol/L (Section 5.4). Polygenic enrichment among clinically ascertained probands, as Trinder 2024 showed across cohorts, remains an alternative explanation I cannot exclude because polygenic scores were not available (Section 5.7)."

**Q5.5 Does the difference depend on reconstructed untreated values?**

*Model answer:* "No. Directly observed values only, +1.09 (0.82 to 1.37, n 1,013); documented pretreatment values only, +1.16 (0.88 to 1.45, n 972); nine back-calculation rules, +1.00 to +1.09, every interval excluding zero; inverse-probability-of-observation weighting, +1.07 (Section 5.4). Reconstruction was needed in 11.3% of probands and 4.7% of relatives, and those rows are not random: 78.4% versus 13.2% of probands with reconstructed versus observed values had prior hard events (Section 5.2). So the primary contrast is the one that does not need reconstruction."

**Q5.6 Regression to the mean: isn't the proband value just the highest value in a noisy series?**

*Model answer:* "Partly, and the revision measured it. Among carriers with two pre-treatment readings, a proband's first reading averaged 2.20 mmol/L (1.62 to 2.76) above the later one, 95 pairs, and a relative's 0.91 (0.43 to 1.42), 84 pairs (Section 5.4). The empirical reference-change value in these pairs was 93% against a within-subject biological CV of about 7.9% for calculated LDL-C. That is compatible with referral-associated measurement selection and regression to the mean, and it cannot be separated from biological change or unrecorded treatment. It is descriptive and post hoc, and no share of the between-group contrast is attributed to it (limitation L01)."

**Q5.7 Is the three-stratum ordering 5.80, 4.76, 3.87 mmol/L a matched comparison?**

*Model answer:* "No. It compares differing allele spectra and is not residue-matched (Section 5.4). Its use is descriptive: it is the phenotype encountered as detection moves from referral, through cascade, to sequencing."

**Q5.8 What does this chapter mean for the 4.9 mmol/L criterion?**

*Model answer:* "Among 715 UK Biobank P/LP carriers with no recorded treatment, 84.5% (81.6 to 86.9) were below 4.9 mmol/L; within the service, 38.1% of probands, 56.9% of relatives and 40.5% of genotype-negative referrals were below it, and untreated LDL-C separated carriers from genotype-negatives with AUC 0.58 on the proband route and 0.76 on the cascade route (Section 5.6). Threshold attainment is a property of the criterion in a specified spectrum. A cascade or genotype-first carrier should not be down-classified for not resembling the proband spectrum."

**Q5.9 Is there a cumulative-exposure story here?**

*Model answer:* "Only weakly and with a poor measure. Baseline LDL-C times attained age, in 3,204 UK Biobank carriers with 137 events, added 0.002 to Harrell's C over age, sex and LDL-C (Section 5.5). That is a single-measurement product, not a life-course integral. The per-mmol associations were 1.15 (Wales, cross-sectional) and 1.21 per mmol/L hazard in UK Biobank, and 1.10 (0.93 to 1.29) on 49 events in exact-allele P/LP carriers. No equivalence was tested; these are not comparable magnitudes."

**Q5.10 What is "selection leakage" and does TUDOR suffer from it?**

*Model answer:* "A predictor that partly reconstructs the case-definition process, such as proband status, referral source or completeness of pedigree recording (Section 5.6). The published TUDOR description included such a term, the Index Effect; the recovered executable object does not (Box 6.1), so the evaluated score cannot leak by that route, but the development population's route mix still shapes its calibration and prevalence."

### 4.6 Chapter 6: Identify (TUDOR)

**Q6.1 What is TUDOR and what is it for?**

*Model answer:* "A treatment-aware elastic-net logistic score, elastic-net mixing 0.5, C = 1.0, saga solver, seed 20260518, with 11 declared inputs of which 10 contribute: reconstructed pretreatment LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides, total cholesterol, non-HDL-C, age, sex, treatment status, family history of CVD (zero variance, coefficient zero), a diabetes-by-LDL interaction and premature CV events (Section 6.2). Its output is a testing-priority score for genotype-confirmed LDLR-mediated FH within a specialist pathway. It is not a diagnosis, a prognostic model or evidence that model-guided testing improves outcomes."

**Q6.2 Give me the head-to-head result and then tell me why it flatters TUDOR.**

*Model answer:* "In 1,274 Welsh participants, 311 carriers, AUC 0.760 (0.727 to 0.790) versus 0.652 for eDLCN, 0.600 FAMCAT, 0.569 Simon Broome, 0.524 MEDPED; NRI against FAMCAT +0.204 (0.141 to 0.266) (Section 6.3). It flatters TUDOR because the comparators were electronic reconstructions deprived of examination, pedigree and primary-care inputs, with unrecorded findings scored as absent (Section 6.4). The counterweight is Akyea 2026: in an Australian tertiary clinic DLCN scored with examination, pedigree and untreated lipids beat FAMCAT, AUROC 0.816 versus 0.748. TUDOR has never been compared with fully scored DLCN or with the Welsh service's own published criteria (limitation L12). The defensible claim is better ranking under shared electronic information."

**Q6.3 Why were the Welsh head-to-head AUC differences reported without a paired test or interval when you own the data?**

*Model answer:* "That is a gap in the source report, and the thesis says so (Section 6.2, Figure 6.1 legend). The paired DeLong test was applied in the local UK Biobank stream (0.747 versus 0.605, difference +0.143). A family-grouped bootstrap of the Welsh paired differences is feasible from the registry alone and is on my plan for before final submission (Part 9)."

**Q6.4 The published TUDOR paper says "ascertainment-aware" with an "Index Effect". Your evaluated object has no such term. Explain, and tell me why there is no corrigendum.**

*Model answer:* "The publication's title and abstract describe an Index Effect encoding proband versus cascade ascertainment. **Q6.5 What happened on transport?**

*Model answer:* "Frozen application to the UK Biobank lipid-clinic-eligible frame, 49,427 participants and 921 carriers, gave AUC 0.669 (0.650 to 0.687); the whole-cohort off-target stress test gave 0.631; refitting coefficients in UK Biobank gave an apparent 0.756, which is local development, not validation (Table 6.3). **Q6.7 What is the workload trade-off?**

### 4.7 Chapter 7: Predict (CALON-5 and the two-term model)

**Q7.1 Describe the two models and why neither is primary.**

*Model answer:* "CALON-5: Cox, five terms, age, male sex, hypertension as recorded antihypertensive medication, diabetes, and log of untreated non-HDL-C over HDL-C; no penalty (Section 7.2). Neither is primary because CALON-5's terms were chosen with knowledge of its transported registry performance and the two-term model was chosen after that (Section 7.1). Both are at high risk of bias on my own PROBAST+AI appraisal (Appendix E.4)."

**Q7.2 "Prespecified ... after the CALON-5 results were known." That is a contradiction. Which is it?**

*Model answer:* "Both words are accurate about different things, and I should not hide behind the phrase. The selection rule was written into the revision protocol of 23 September 2026 before it was run; but it was written after CALON-5's transported results were known, so the whole exercise is conditional on them. **Q7.3 Twenty-eight events. Why report the registry fit at all?**

*Model answer:* "The registry fit is exploratory and labelled so; the interpreted reverse direction is the all-carrier fit with 52 events (Section 7.3). **Q7.4 Give me the discrimination and calibration headline, then the mechanism.**

*Model answer:* "Frozen ten-year concordance 0.699 (0.611 to 0.790) into the registry and 0.698 (0.609 to 0.778) out of it; across seven estimable cells 0.698 to 0.722, every interval overlapping (Section 7.5). **Q7.6 The reconstruction equation has R-squared 0.0975 and a slope of 0.33. Why is that acceptable?**

*Model answer:* "It is acceptable only for what it is used for. It targets the guideline-specified untreated quantity at group level for the 24.7% of biobank carriers on therapy; it compresses the treated between-person spread and was fitted mostly on non-carrier pairs, so it is not accurate for an individual (Sections 7.2, 7.10, Appendix D.3). The error is plausibly differential, because treatment starts in response to earlier lipids. The registry uses recorded values, so the two cohorts measure the exposure differently, which is a stated limitation. The stronger design is documented pre-treatment samples and propagation of a distribution of plausible untreated values through fitting; the propagation protocol is frozen but was not run (Appendix D.4)."

**Q7.7 Missingness in the registry was informative. What did you do?**

*Model answer:* "Nothing to repair it, deliberately. Diabetes status was recorded for 54% of standardised registry participants; those without it had about half the hazard, age- and sex-adjusted 0.40 (0.21 to 0.77), so missing-at-random is doubtful and I did not impute (Section 7.4). *Model answer:* "Substantially for absolute risk, less for ranking. ### 4.8 Chapter 8: Discussion, sex section, limitations

**Q8.1 Where do the guidelines sit relative to your conclusion?**

*Model answer:* "Nothing here challenges LDL-C's primacy. The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety guideline rates apoB measurement COR 2a in treated adults and 2b in untreated adults including inherited disorders; the 2025 ESC/EAS focused update makes no apoB recommendation (Section 8.1). FH-specific risk scores get COR 2b for short-term risk, and UK guidance advises against general-population tools in FH (Section 8.6). My results are consistent with all of that."

**Q8.2 The sex section rests on one population study. Why is it in an FH thesis?**

*Model answer:* "As a bounded limit on single-marker reading, not as an FH result (Sections 1.5, 8.4). In 273,036 UK Biobank women with an age-matched male reference, at age 58 an apoB of at least 1.2 g/L corresponded to ten-year MACE risk of 10.88% in men and 4.34% in postmenopausal women; a sex-neutral internal model over-predicted in women, O/E 0.65 to 0.78, and under-predicted in men, 1.19. LDLR carriers rose +11.5% across menopause against +15.3% in non-carriers, which is compatible with Da Roza 2026's larger absolute rise in monogenic FH because the metrics and starting levels differ (limitation L18). *Model answer:* "Table 8.3, top five: no untouched evaluation cohort and both settings in the UK (L04); few events and informative pedigrees, a registry fit on 28 events with informative missingness (L15); treatment reconstruction as a non-random, model-based surrogate (L02, L08); the grading result estimated only in carriers with a documented untreated value (L16); registry event times from recorded ages with heavy censoring (L07). **Q8.4 What in this thesis changes practice tomorrow?**

*Model answer:* "One thing, and it is a recording change, not a treatment change: record the first untreated LDL-C with date and method as a fixed field and grade severity on it; where none exists, say so rather than let the on-treatment value stand in (Section 8.6). Everything else is 'better understood' or 'warrants service evaluation'. No change in care has been tested."

**Q8.5 What is the 'chapter-removal test'?**

*Model answer:* "Section 8.3. Each chapter carries weight no other supplies: without Chapter 4 the particle condition rests on general-population evidence; without 5 the route condition is asserted, not measured; without 6 the treatment condition has no identification test; without 7 the separation of ranking from absolute risk has no FH demonstration. The strongest simpler explanation is that these are five familiar principles established separately, and the thesis accepts it: its contribution is their measurement together in linked genotyped cohorts."

## Part 6. Genetic-medicine questions

**Q6A. What is the "operational LDLR-carrier flag", and why does it give 1 in 142 when FH prevalence is nearer 1 in 250 to 300?**

*Model answer:* "It is a broad variant flag in the locked extract that selected 3,540 of 501,936 participants, and Appendix B.2 says explicitly that this frequency exceeds published estimates of pathogenic FH-variant prevalence and lacked identifiers for complete re-adjudication of pathogenicity, zygosity or functional class. That is why it is never labelled HeFH and why Chapter 7 restricts to ClinVar P/LP single-variant carriers, 1,009 of the 3,209 flagged participants free of ASCVD (Section 7.4). Among the 2,398 untreated flagged carriers in Chapter 5, 29.8% were P/LP and 46.3% VUS (Section 5.6)."

**Q6B. ClinVar P/LP by database, not expert adjudication. How different are the two cohorts' calls?**

*Model answer:* "Materially. At two or more review stars, 69.0% of the 1,009 biobank P/LP variants qualified against 97.7% of 954 registry variants; zygosity is recorded in neither cohort (Section 7.4, limitation L09). The P/LP fraction was 31.4% in the biobank and 57.7% in the registry, which is itself an ascertainment gradient in the genetic label. **Q6C. Why combine LDLR and APOB variants in Chapter 4 when APOB p.(Arg3527Gln) typically gives a milder phenotype?**

*Model answer:* "The Chapter 4 frame is an operational rare LDLR/APOB variant rule inherited from the source-locked package and its results are frame-specific (Section 4.2). The thesis does not report gene-stratified estimates for Chapter 4 and, at 75 events, could not do so with precision. I accept that APOB and LDLR carriers may differ in particle composition and that a gene-stratified sensitivity should be listed among the resolving-study requirements. Chapter 7 tested LDLR versus other gene as an interaction with the lipid term and the registry cell had too few events (Section 7.13)."

**Q6D. What does residue matching actually control, and what does variant identity explain?**

*Model answer:* "Position on the receptor, not the substitution or its function (Section 5.2). Variant identity explains a small share of LDL-C variance: intraclass correlation about 0.145, leave-one-variant-out 0.127 to 0.149 in the TUDOR analyses, and carriers of the same LDLR variants had LDL-C 1.36 mmol/L lower in UK Biobank than in the Welsh clinic (minus 2.03 to minus 0.76). A predicted measure of variant function tracked the untreated LDL-C stratum in relatives, rho 0.26, but not in probands, 0.02, a post hoc pattern consistent with selection acting on the phenotype (Section 5.2)."

**Q6E. Penetrance: does a cascade-detected relative with LDL-C 3.8 mmol/L have FH?**

*Model answer:* "Yes, in the molecular sense, and the thesis's interpretive rule is that a lower LDL-C observed through a different route should not overturn an expert-adjudicated familial LDLR diagnosis or imply low cumulative burden (Section 5.7). Expressivity, recognition and prognosis are separated deliberately (Section 5.1). Management follows molecular and clinical context and guidance, not a manufactured proband value."

**Q6F. Polygenic risk: what did you find and what does it mean?**

*Model answer:* "Association without incremental ranking. Chapter 4's exploratory coronary PRS: 1.43 per SD (1.12 to 1.84) with concordance 0.569 and 64.1% missing. Consistent with Paquette 2026's FH result of hazard ratio 1.77 and a C change from 0.746 to 0.750 (Section 2.9). Polygenic enrichment among probands remains the unexcluded competing explanation in Chapter 5."

**Q6G. Ancestry?**

**Q6H. Incidental and direct-to-consumer findings?**

## Part 7. Biostatistics rapid-fire (one-line answers)

- **Events per parameter for CALON-5?** 14.8 in UK Biobank, 5.6 in the registry P/LP fit; floor was 10 (Section 7.3).
- **Optimism?** Whole-procedure bootstrap: apparent 0.6972, optimism 0.0209, corrected 0.6763; slope corrected 0.901 (Section 7.3).
- **Proportional hazards?** 0 of 10 tests at p under 0.05 for CALON-5, little power on 52 registry events; Chapter 4's diagnostic object not recovered (Sections 7.3, 4.5).
- **Why cause-specific Cox rather than Fine-Gray for the association?** The estimand was the rate among the event-free; Fine-Gray answers the cumulative-incidence question and is reported only as a sensitivity (Table 3.3).
- **Why Aalen-Johansen?** One minus Kaplan-Meier overestimates absolute risk when competing death is censored (Section 3.5).
- **Why no imputation in Chapter 7?** Missingness associated with the outcome, hazard ratio 0.40, so MAR doubtful (Section 7.4).
- **Why 20 imputations in the discordance paper?** Source study choice for 40% apoB missingness; imputed apoB is not an assay (Section 4.4).
- **What determines the width of a concordance interval?** Number of events and, for paired comparisons, correlation between scores, not cohort size (Section 3.8).
- **Twenty-event floor?** Comparisons below 20 events at a declared horizon are not interpreted (Section 3.8).
- **Sequential interval rule for updating?** Baseline and slope where the slope interval excludes one; baseline alone where only the O/E interval does; otherwise none; not a closed testing procedure (Section 3.8).
- **Why no equivalence claims?** No prespecified margin; an interval including zero is 'undemonstrated, not demonstrated absent' (Section 3.7.1, reference 80).
- **NRI weaknesses?** Sensitive to prevalence, category definitions and miscalibration; secondary only (Sections 6.3, 6.7, reference 88).
- **Brier score?** Overall error at a horizon combining discrimination and calibration; not a calibration metric; small when events are rare (Section 3.8).
- **Wild cluster bootstrap?** Rademacher, 1,999 pedigree resamples, same interval 0.83 to 1.36 as the clustered SE (Section 5.3).
- **Why is the dummy-variable df correction relevant?** With 488 pedigree fixed effects it counts each as a parameter and widens the within-pedigree interval to minus 0.06 to 0.85 (Section 5.3).
- **Why is the +0.052 Montreal win fragile?** Lower limit +0.0003 on 20 events, and it did not resolve under the earlier hypertension definition (Section 7.8).
- **Why did you change the hypertension definition?** To recorded medication only, on 24 September 2026, before any refit result was seen; the earlier definition is a sensitivity (Section 7.2).
- **Time-dependent AUC versus Harrell C?** Both reported: 0.718 and 0.713 at ten years alongside 0.699 and 0.698 (Section 7.5).
- **Why is target refit AUC 0.756 not comparable with frozen 0.669?** Different model state; outcomes informed the coefficients (Section 6.5).
- **What is 'calculability'?** Rows on which an instrument can be computed as published; SAFEHEART-RE 26 registry rows with one event (Table 7.3).

## Part 8. Lipid-medicine rapid-fire (one-line answers)

- **Why does LDL-C under-grade after treatment?** Median 6.1 untreated versus 3.6 on treatment in 261 registry pairs; 66.3% drop a stratum (Section 7.7).
- **Why not just back-calculate?** Because every convention has MAE around 1.2 mmol/L and correlation 0.09 to 0.32 with documented values (Part 1, P11).
- **Where is Friedewald least reliable?** Below 1.4 mmol/L the triglyceride term is 47% of the calculated value (Section 7.7).
- **Non-HDL-C alternative in Chapter 5?** +1.67 mmol/L crude, +1.44 age- and sex-adjusted; same-visit differences 1.64 and 1.49 (Section 5.4).
- **Does a severity cut-point rank risk?** Poorly: concordance 0.552 to 0.593 for 4.9 mmol/L alone; but the event-rate ratio above versus below was 2.97 in the registry and 1.61 in the biobank (Sections 7.5, 7.7).
- **Reaching goal means low lifetime exposure?** No: of 51 registry patients at the 2.6 goal with a recorded pre-treatment value, 54.9% had been at or above 4.9 (Section 7.7).
- **Lp(a)?** Modifier, not subtractable component; measured in only 143 of 1,904 Welsh carriers in Chapter 5; Lp(a) of at least 125 nmol/L over CALON-5 gave 0.88 (0.40 to 1.94) (Sections 5.7, 7.9).
- **Should clinics measure apoB?** Where the question concerns particle burden; guidance already supports it in treated adults (COR 2a); the 0.31 ratio threshold should not be adopted (Section 8.6).
- **Which ratio direction did the paper use?** apoB/LDL-C in g/mmol, threshold 0.31; other literature uses LDL-C/apoB as a size proxy (Section 2.5).
- **Diabetes and TUDOR?** AUC 0.648 Wales, 0.642 UK Biobank: a metabolically challenging setting, not a subgroup licence (Section 6.5).
- **Cascade versus proband age?** Median 60.2 versus 38.6 years; treated 89.0% versus 67.5%; prior ASCVD 21.3% versus 6.9% (Table 5.1).
- **Minimum data set a service needs for any FH risk score?** Untreated LDL-C, Lp(a), BMI, diabetes and hypertension status, dated events (Section 8.6).
- **Menopause and FH LDL-C?** LDLR carriers +11.5% versus +15.3% percentage rise from a higher base; Da Roza 2026 found a larger absolute rise in monogenic FH; compatible (Section 8.4).
- **Pregnancy?** Klevmoen 2021 documented loss of statin-treatment years; adds untreated exposure current status cannot summarise (Section 2.11).
